How a floor
is built.

  1. Prepare

    The concrete is cleaned, ground and repaired so the resin can grip. Everything that follows depends on this step.

  2. Prime

    A primer soaks into the surface and seals it, giving the base coat something solid to bond to.

  3. Base coat

    A solid, even coat sets the colour that sits underneath the finish.

  4. Pour the finish

    Metallic pigments are poured and worked by hand to create the swirls and depth. This is where each floor becomes one of a kind.

  5. Seal

    A clear, hard-wearing topcoat locks everything in and gives the floor its gloss. We'll tell you when it's safe to walk on and when it's fully cured.

Flake and quartz floors follow the same build, with flakes or quartz in place of the metallic pour.

Questions,answered.

Can resin go over my existing floor?

In many cases yes, including concrete and some tiled floors. We'll check the surface and tell you what preparation it needs before we quote.

Is an epoxy floor slippery?

A gloss finish can be slippery when wet. We can add an anti-slip finish where it matters, such as garages, steps and wet areas. Quartz finishes are naturally grippy.

How long does it take?

It depends on the size of the floor, the finish and the preparation needed. We'll give you a clear timeline with your quote, including when you can walk on it and when it's fully cured.

Can I have it in a kitchen or living area?

Yes. Metallic epoxy is a popular choice for kitchens, utility rooms and open-plan living spaces because it's seamless and easy to keep clean.

Do you work with businesses?

Yes. We install commercial epoxy floors for shops, kitchens, workshops and warehouses. Tell us how the space is used and we'll recommend the right system.
